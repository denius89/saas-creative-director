# Blind A/B protocol

## Benchmark set

Use six to ten briefs spanning workflow automation, analytics, collaboration, AI feature launch, security/compliance, and a product with limited usable screenshots. Freeze each brief, evidence registry, asset pack, target duration, and audience. Do not use a Tier A source work as the client brief.

For each brief create:

- A: the 0.6 baseline storyboard and Figma output;
- B: the Motion Intelligence workflow with the same facts and production constraints.

Randomize left/right order and remove file names, version labels, prompts, model names, and source brands that reveal the condition. Show equivalent contact sheets, representative state strips, and the same handoff fields.

## Review

Use at least three reviewers across creative direction, motion production, and product marketing. Each reviewer scores the rubric independently before discussion and chooses which output they would take into production. Capture confidence and the reason for preference.

Resolve blocking factual or copy-provenance issues before computing preference. Report median score per dimension, preference count, disagreement, and edit time for a producer to fix the top three issues. Do not report a single blended modernity number without the component scores.

## Acceptance target

For the MVP candidate:

- B wins production preference on at least two thirds of judged briefs;
- no decline in product truth or evidence integrity;
- median contemporary relevance and production clarity each improve by at least one rubric point;
- no destructive or duplicate Figma imports;
- a producer can identify every required state, transition anchor, source boundary, and locked decision.

Six to ten briefs are a directional product benchmark, not a universal scientific claim. Keep raw anonymized ratings and benchmark revisions so later releases can be compared honestly.
