# Reference curation

## Concrete references

Create one record per inspected work. Preserve the canonical URL, source ID, creator/studio, project type, inspection method, inspection date, confidence, access limitations, and timestamped scene observations. Unknown fields remain unknown.

Use Tier A studios as benchmark seeds. Competitor research answers market and positioning questions; visual-reference research answers communication and production questions. Keep their artifacts and retrieval indexes separate even when one work is relevant to both.

## Scene observations

A scene observation records its time range, communication job, viewer state before and after, product mechanism, focal hierarchy, composition relationship, motion function, transition anchor, asset type, evidence boundary, and visible limitation. It does not store downloaded brand assets unless rights and project policy allow that.

## Derived patterns

Promote a pattern only after abstracting the problem and mechanism away from the source expression. Prefer support from more than one independently inspected work. A single-source idea stays experimental. Record the source observation IDs, counterexamples, when to use, when to avoid, non-copy rule, version, and freshness review date.

## Gold benchmark promotion

A benchmark record becomes gold only when two reviewers watch the complete work, add timestamped annotations independently, reconcile disagreements, and confirm the non-copy boundary. Portfolio claims and campaign metrics remain attributed claims; they do not prove that a visual choice caused an outcome.
